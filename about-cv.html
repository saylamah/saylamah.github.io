Professional summary

Scientific contribution before professional chronology

The work connects physical chemistry, thermodynamics, chemical kinetics, transport phenomena, reactor and CFD modelling, experiments and process engineering. The objective is not simply to describe a technical effect, but to determine what governs it, how it can be validated and whether it can be transferred responsibly to a practical system.

The professional path spans academic research, industrial R&D, combustion and emissions development, thermochemical-process engineering, experimental and pilot-oriented development, technical project leadership and scientific communication.

Recommendations are assessed against reproducibility, material and energy efficiency, operability, safety, evidence quality, scale-dependent risk and realistic industrial value.

Core professional proposition

Reliable technology development requires chemistry, transport, equipment behaviour, operating conditions, evidence quality and scale to be evaluated together.

Models, experiments, process concepts and pilot systems are therefore treated as connected decision tools rather than isolated technical activities.

Professional evidence model

Professional development path

A career developed across complementary technical scales

The path combines fundamental research, computational science, experimental development, process engineering and technical project leadership.

  1. Fundamental and doctoral research

    Oxidation, autoignition and combustion chemistry

    Doctoral-level research addressed the oxidation and autoignition of alkanes, aromatic compounds and their mixtures under elevated-pressure conditions, supported by detailed chemical-kinetic modelling.

  2. Combustion, emissions and particle research

    Flames, engines, soot and pollutant formation

    Subsequent work expanded into premixed and high-pressure flames, HCCI systems, engine pollutant formation, soot and nanoparticle inception, experimental diagnostics and reaction-network analysis.

  3. Industrial experimental development

    Burner systems, instrumentation and emissions evaluation

    Industrial R&D work added test-rig integration, sensors, acquisition and control interfaces, planned testing, technical reporting and design-oriented interpretation.

  4. Thermochemical and process development

    Pyrolysis, biomass, syngas and process concepts

    The scope broadened to pyrolysis-process development, plant trials, process calculations, technical documentation, plant-concept engineering and wider thermochemical systems.

  5. Industrial projects and technology development

    Customer projects, coordination and applied engineering

    Recent work combines project planning and control, multidisciplinary coordination, customer and supplier interfaces, technical assessment and the translation of scientific principles into development decisions.

  6. Current professional direction

    R&D leadership, specialist roles and defined consulting

    The current direction prioritizes technically suitable roles and assignments requiring scientific depth, engineering judgement, validation discipline, process-development capability and implementation awareness.
