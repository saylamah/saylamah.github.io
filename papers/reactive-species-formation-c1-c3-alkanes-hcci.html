Independent research summary

This study uses single-zone homogeneous charge compression ignition simulations to examine the selective formation of reactive intermediates during the lean oxidation of methane, ethane, and propane. Compression ratio, engine speed, equivalence ratio, intake temperature, and selected reactive additions are assessed to identify operating conditions that favour oxygenated hydrocarbons, hydrogen peroxide, and ethylene. The analysis shows that intermediate-species production is strongly dependent on the chosen thermochemical and engine operating conditions, with compression ratio defining a particularly narrow useful operating window. The work also evaluates the broader potential of HCCI operation as a route for coupling fuel conversion with the in-situ generation of chemically useful intermediates.
