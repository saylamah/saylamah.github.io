Independent research summary

This study introduces degree centrality as a graph-theoretic criterion for identifying principal species in detailed combustion reaction networks. The method is integrated into a dynamic adaptive chemistry workflow in which locally important species are selected according to the thermochemical state at each simulation time step or computational cell. The results distinguish network centrality from conventional importance measures and show that highly connected or active species are not always identical to the species required for accurate predictive mechanism reduction.
