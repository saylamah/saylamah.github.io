Independent research summary

This study evaluates how burner diameter and surrounding shielding flows influence measurements and simulations of near-threshold sooting premixed flames. Two-dimensional modelling and experiments at atmospheric pressure and 10 bar were used to assess lateral species diffusion and heat-transfer effects for methane-air and ethylene-air flames. The results show that these effects are most pronounced near the soot-formation threshold and at atmospheric pressure. Larger burner diameters and reactive flame shielding better preserve quasi-one-dimensional axial temperature, species, and soot-volume-fraction profiles, providing practical guidance for the design and interpretation of soot-formation experiments.
