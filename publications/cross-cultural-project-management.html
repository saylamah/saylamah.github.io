Cross-Cultural Project Management: Insights from Five Global Contexts

Author: Ahmad Saylam

Document type: Preprint

Year: 2025

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19768789

Zenodo record: https://zenodo.org/records/19768789

Abstract

In an era of globalization and digital transformation, success in professional environments requires more than technical excellence—it demands a deep understanding of the cultural, organizational, and hierarchical contexts that shape behavior and expectations.

This comparative study investigates the influence of national cultures (USA, China, Germany, Russia, and Arab countries), work domains (academic and industrial R&D, management, manufacturing, and learning), and responsibility levels (from researchers to executives) on the definition of project goals, team composition, outcome evaluation, and the selection of management methodologies (classic, agile, hybrid).

It highlights the strategic implications of choosing between specialized, multidisciplinary, and global-view professionals, while exploring the cultural tensions, integration challenges, and synergistic advantages of bringing together such diverse profiles.

With updated citations, this paper proposes context-sensitive recommendations—globally and specifically for Arab countries and post-conflict Syria—emphasizing adaptability, inclusive leadership, and cross-disciplinary collaboration as drivers for sustainable progress and innovation.
