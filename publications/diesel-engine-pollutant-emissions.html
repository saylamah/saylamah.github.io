Methodology to reduce diesel engine pollutant emissions

Author: Ahmad Saylam

Document type: Preprint / Technical Paper

Year: 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19438401

Zenodo record: https://zenodo.org/records/19438401

Abstract

Due to climate change and health issues, more restrictive legislations concerning pollutant emissions of diesel engines are being introduced worldwide. A novel approach to reduce the pollutant emissions of CO, NOx and unburned hydrocarbons (HC) in diesel engines is proposed. It is based on fuelling diesel engines with blends of selective additive mixtures and conventional commercial diesel fuel.

In order to prove the feasibility of such an approach, blends of additive mixtures and a diesel fuel surrogate of n-dodecane and m-xylene are numerically investigated using a homogeneous multi-zones reactor approach and detailed chemistries, covering a wide blending range of air–fuel ratio (AFR) 9.8–29.4 and engine speed 1000–2000 rpm.

The employed mixture of additives considerably reduces emissions of CO and HC, up to 60%, especially for rich conditions at AFR = 9.8, with generally very slight reduction of NOx emissions and decrease in produced engine power for all blending ratios.

The reduction of NOx emissions is thus reachable by the accessibility to operate the engine at low compression ratio while blending the additive mixture. The proposed approach needs to be validated experimentally and coupled with other emissions-reduction technologies.
